Nathan Jacobs

Professor of Computer Science & Engineering
Vice Provost for Artificial Intelligence
Director, Multimodal Vision Research Laboratory
Washington University in St. Louis

I develop new computer vision and AI methods for understanding the physical and natural world. My lab's research spans multimodal representation learning, geospatial foundation models, and generative modeling, with models that learn from satellite imagery, ground-level photos, sound, and text. We apply these methods with collaborators on problems in agriculture, ecology, forestry, air quality, natural hazards, and public health. As Vice Provost for Artificial Intelligence, I help make that kind of collaboration work across an entire university.

What I Work On

Novel AI methods

My lab develops computer vision and machine learning methods that connect many ways of observing the world, from satellite imagery and ground-level photos to sound and text. Recent examples include TaxaBind, a shared embedding space for six kinds of ecological data, and TerraDiT, a generative Earth model that won a best paper award at ECCV 2026's TerraBytes workshop. We publish at CVPR, ICCV, ECCV, NeurIPS, and WACV, and release code, models, and data.

AI for impact

Much of our most consequential work is part of larger collaborative efforts, where we contribute AI methods and models:

  • Agriculture: Fields of The World and the first global 10 m field boundary map
  • Forests: deadtrees.earth and global tree mortality mapping
  • Air quality: global fine particulate matter estimation
  • Natural hazards: landslide mapping from lidar
  • Public health: built environment and health

AI across a university

As Vice Provost for Artificial Intelligence at WashU, I help lead university-wide strategy for AI in research and education: shared AI infrastructure, support for researchers in every discipline who want to use AI in their work, AI in teaching and learning, and partnerships with industry, government, and foundations.
